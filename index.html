The Craft

Six months. Four hands. Zero shortcuts.

From billet to first chord, every step happens on one bench — the way it’s been done for three hundred years.

  1. 01

    Tonewood selection

    We tap-tune every top before it’s cut, listening for the ring that predicts a great soundboard. Our wood library seasons for a decade before it touches a blade.

  2. 02

    Hand-carved bracing

    Braces are split, not sawn, then scalloped by chisel while the top is flexed and tapped — tuning stiffness to the individual piece of wood, gram by gram.

  3. 03

    French polish finish

    Hundreds of whisper-thin shellac layers applied by hand pad. The thinnest finish in the business — the top vibrates as if it were bare wood.

  4. 04

    Setup & voicing

    Strings on, we play each instrument for two weeks — adjusting saddle, nut, and neck relief until it feels effortless from the first fret to the fourteenth.

Our Story

A family name, carried in every soundhole.

Mateo D’Cruz built his first guitar at nineteen from a church pew someone was throwing away. Twenty-seven years later the tools are better, the wood is better, but the obsession is the same: build the instrument he wished he could have afforded as a kid.

We build twelve guitars a year. Not because we can’t build more — because we won’t. Every top is tuned by ear, every neck shaped to its player, and every instrument leaves the shop only when it sounds like a D’Cruz.

“A factory can make a good guitar. Only a person can make your guitar.” — Mateo D’Cruz, founder & luthier
